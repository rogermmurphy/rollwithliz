How to diversify your book as a carrier

Working with multiple brokers and direct customers · 7 min read

Almost every carrier I've worked with who ran into trouble had the same thing in common: too much of their revenue came from one place. One broker, one lane, one big customer. It feels great while it lasts — until that broker cuts rates, loses the account, or just goes quiet for two weeks. Now your trucks are sitting and the payments aren't.

Diversifying your book is how you stop letting any single relationship control whether you make money. Here's how to think about it.

Why concentration is the silent killer

A useful rule of thumb: if any one customer or broker is more than about 30–40% of your revenue, you're exposed. When one account is that big, you stop negotiating from strength. You take the cheap load because you can't afford to say no, and you tolerate slow pay because you can't afford to lose them. That's not a business — that's a job with a lot of risk attached.

The three buckets every carrier should fill

A healthy book usually pulls from three sources, and each plays a different role:

1. Load boards — for speed, not strategy

Boards (DAT, Truckstop, and the like) are great for filling a gap today and for getting to know new lanes. But spot rates swing hard, and a board full of strangers is the opposite of a relationship. Use them to stay loaded and to prospect — not as your whole plan.

2. Broker relationships — for consistency

A handful of brokers who know your trucks, your lanes, and that you run clean and on time will feed you steady freight. The goal isn't one great broker — it's five or six who each give you regular loads, so a slow week from one is just a normal week from the others.

3. Direct customers — for margin and stability

Going direct to shippers cuts out the middleman's cut, which usually means better rates and stickier relationships. It takes more legwork — you're doing your own sales and handling your own paperwork — but even one or two direct accounts can anchor your week and lift your average rate per mile.

How to actually build the mix

  • Track where your money comes from. Once a month, list your top payers and what percent of revenue each one is. You can't fix concentration you can't see.
  • Add before you need to. The time to court a new broker is when you're busy and can be choosy — not the week your main account dries up.
  • Be the carrier people want to keep. On-time pickups, clean BOLs, good communication, and fast paperwork get you called first. Reputation is diversification you can't buy.
  • Protect a lane you own. When you find a direct customer or a dedicated round-trip that works, treat it like gold — that's the freight that carries you through the soft season.
